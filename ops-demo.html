Live demo: wipe an ESP32 and write a meteo station back — MCP tools vs bare hands

One board, one job — erase the flash, install the firmware, deploy the meteo station's main.py, verify — done two ways. First with bare pyserial/esptool and no harness. Then through the ironharness MCP server: the same operations, plus the two things a bare script cannot give you — a gate that refused to destroy the board until explicitly told to, and a journal that replays every step.

The esp_erase call was made without the opt-in flag first. The tool refused in 0.0 s and journaled its own refusal. The bare-hands road has no such concept: the first run of the script simply erased the flash.

First run of this road FAILED at verify — and the lesson is the point: pyserial asserts DTR/RTS on open, and on this board's reset circuit a held DTR blocks the RTS→EN pair, so the board goes silent (0 bytes in two 8-second windows). The harness kills DTR/RTS right after open; the bare script has to know that. It also has to know the Ctrl-B/Ctrl-C×2/Ctrl-A dance, the hex-chunk raw-REPL protocol and the cooked-mode limits. Evidence trail of road A: whatever the script happened to write into its own log files.

143 journal events (who / when / what / answer): every step — including the refusal and every hex chunk in both directions — replays from journal.jsonl. The meteo station was verified by an independent full round-trip, not by trusting the put's return value.

What the comparison actually says

  • The work is identical, the timings are comparable (road A ~56 s of active steps vs road B ~115 s; the difference is the hex protocol of put/get and two server spawns). MCP is not sold as faster here — and this page is not going to pretend otherwise.
  • Safety: road B refused to wipe until explicitly unlocked, and recorded the refusal; road A erased the flash on its first launch — no gate exists in that world.
  • Demonstrability: road B leaves 143 replayable events; road A leaves self-written logs that are easy to not write.
  • Knowledge: road A requires the board folklore (the DTR/RTS quirk that failed the first run, serial-line polarities, the raw-REPL hex protocol); road B requires tool names and arguments. That folklore-absorption is exactly what io-core is for.

Honesty notes

  • One demo run per road (after road A's failed first attempt, which is kept and described above — it is the argument, not noise). Not a benchmark; the benchmark lives next door.
  • The board ended the demo in a healthy state: meteo station byte-for-byte identical to the canonical source, firmware v1.27.0 (the same build), and it was never left without a main.py after the roads started.
  • The station prints a boot-time OSError ENODEV at its OLED init — the display is physically off the I2C bus; that is the documented expected signature of this bench, not a failure.
